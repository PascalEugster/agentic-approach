# People to follow

A short list. I try to keep this under 10 names — more than that and I stop reading any of them carefully.

## Practitioners

### Simon Willison — simonwillison.net, @simonw
The most thorough public documentor of agentic coding in practice. Writes nearly daily. Notes, experiments, failures, patterns. If I can only follow one person on this topic, it's him.

### Dex Horthy — HumanLayer, @dexhorthy
Pioneer of context engineering and QRSPI. His talks are where the structural vocabulary for agent work comes from.

### Mitchell Hashimoto — @mitchellh
Coined "harness engineering." His framing of the harness-as-OS shaped how I think about the layer around the model.

### Geoffrey Huntley — ghuntley.com, @geoffreyhuntley
Ralph Loop, Gas Town, and the "agents as tight loops" school. His writing is opinionated and sometimes extreme, which is exactly why it's useful.

### Andrej Karpathy — @karpathy
Sets the discourse. "LLMs as OS," `autoresearch`, MicroGPT, "vibe coding" — if Karpathy names something, the rest of the field picks up the term within weeks.

## Strategy and framing

### Ethan Mollick — oneusefulthing.org, @emollick
Best translator of AI capability into business implications. Wharton professor, readable, calibrated. The one I send to people who want to understand "what should we do about this?" without getting lost in technical detail.

### Tobi Lütke — @tobi
Shopify CEO, practicing engineer. His public experiments (the Liquid template engine autoresearch run, the "AI before headcount" mandate) are concrete examples of what aggressive adoption looks like from the inside.

### swyx / Shawn Wang — latent.space, @swyx
Host of the most important AI engineering podcast and conference. Good taste in guests, good taste in topics, strong signal-to-noise ratio.

## Product direction

### Boris Cherny — Head of Claude Code, Anthropic
Defines where Claude Code is heading. Worth watching for signals about what the harness is going to be able to do next quarter.

### David Soria Parra — MCP lead maintainer
The protocol's future runs through him. His roadmap talks are the best source for where MCP is going.

## The discipline

> *Follow the people whose work you'd implement. Don't follow the people whose takes you'd quote.*

Quote-followers produce Twitter. Implementation-followers produce changes to how I work. This list is heavily biased toward the latter.

## Unfollow rules

I'll drop someone from this list when:

- They start tweeting more about the discourse than about the work.
- Their predictions start missing in ways they don't acknowledge.
- I notice I'm reading them out of habit instead of usefulness.

Maintaining attention as a resource is itself a form of context engineering.
